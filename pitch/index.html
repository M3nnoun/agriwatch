Parametric: the measurement is the trigger — no claim, no inspector. That's why it's instant and cheap.

Here's how. We pull crop-health imagery from Sentinel-2 satellites, combine it with rainfall and forecast data from NASA and Open-Meteo, and score drought risk using published FAO thresholds for each crop. If the score crosses the line, Claude writes the alert and the payout fires automatically. The key word is parametric: the measurement itself is the trigger. No claim form, no inspector visiting the field. That's why AgriWatch can be both instant and cheap enough to reach everyone.

How big is this? The global agricultural protection gap is over a trillion dollars in uninsured climate risk. And here's the smart part of the model: the farmer is the user, but the farmer usually isn't the payer. Governments and development agencies subsidize premiums. Co-ops and microfinance institutions pay because it de-risks the loans they've already made. And reinsurers underwrite the payout pool while we earn a technology and distribution fee. The buyers are already actively looking for exactly this.

The model. Because payouts are automatic, premiums can be tiny — around three dollars a season for an individual farmer, two-fifty per farmer at co-op scale, and custom programmes for governments and insurers. We make money two ways: a share of the premium, and a per-farm software fee. And the economics get better with scale, because the satellite and AI costs are basically fixed whether we cover a thousand farms or ten million.
